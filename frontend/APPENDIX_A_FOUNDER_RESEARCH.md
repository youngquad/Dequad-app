## 1. Research Objectives

The founder set out to test five core hypotheses before committing to the Dequad product:

1. **H1** — UK university students experience loneliness and mental health difficulty at levels above what existing services can meet.
2. **H2** — Existing UK solutions (counselling, generic apps, university apps) are perceived by students as insufficient.
3. **H3** — UK students would value a **`.ac.uk`-verified peer connection** product over generic dating apps.
4. **H4** — UK welfare teams need **anonymised early-warning data** but currently lack it.
5. **H5** — UK welfare leads would adopt a B2B safeguarding alert tool if it integrated with their existing workflows.

### 4.2 Welfare Team Pain Points (Recurring Themes)

1. **Reactive, not proactive** — they only learn about students in crisis after disclosure or incident
2. **No anonymised aggregate visibility** of student wellbeing trends
3. **Counselling capacity outstripped** by demand (consistent 4–6 week waits)
4. **Cost-of-living crisis** intensifying caseloads with no extra budget
5. **OfS B3 pressure** to demonstrate measurable welfare interventions
6. **Procurement risk-aversion** — must have UK GDPR, ICO, UK data residency
